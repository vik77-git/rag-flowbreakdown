NORTHWIND ANALYTICS INFORMATION SECURITY POLICY
Version 3.2 - Fictional sample document for demonstration only

1. Purpose and Scope
This policy defines the mandatory security controls for all Northwind Analytics
production systems, internal tools and third-party integrations. It applies to every
employee, contractor and automated service account that accesses company systems.
Exceptions require written approval from the Security Review Board and must be
recorded in the exception register with an expiry date of no more than 90 days.

2. Data Classification
Northwind classifies data into four tiers: Public, Internal, Confidential and
Restricted. Restricted data includes customer financial records and authentication
secrets. Confidential and Restricted data must be encrypted at rest using AES-256 and
in transit using TLS 1.2 or higher. Internal data may be stored on managed endpoints
provided full-disk encryption is enabled. Public data has no encryption requirement but
still requires integrity controls before publication.

3. Access Control
3.1 Least Privilege
Access is granted on a least-privilege basis. Every entitlement must map to a documented
role. Standing administrative access to production is prohibited; administrators request
time-bound elevation that expires automatically after four hours.

3.2 Access Reviews
Team leads review entitlements every quarter. Access for departing personnel must be
revoked within four hours of termination. Service account credentials are reviewed every
90 days and rotated at least annually.

3.3 Segregation of Duties
The engineer who authors a production change may not be the sole approver of that change.
Release approval and deployment execution are separated for all Restricted systems.

4. Authentication
4.1 Password Requirements
Passwords must be at least 14 characters, screened against a breached-password list, and
may not be reused across systems. Password rotation is not forced on a schedule; rotation
is required on suspicion of compromise.

4.2 Multi-Factor Authentication
Multi-factor authentication is mandatory for all remote access, all administrative
consoles and any system holding Confidential or Restricted data. Phishing-resistant
factors such as FIDO2 security keys are required for privileged accounts. SMS-based
one-time codes are permitted only as a temporary fallback and must be disabled by the end
of the fiscal year. Session tokens expire after 12 hours of inactivity and are bound to
the issuing device.

4.3 Authentication Risks
The principal authentication risks identified in the last assessment are credential
stuffing against the customer portal, phishing of employee single sign-on credentials,
session token theft through cross-site scripting, and weakly protected service account
keys stored in source repositories. Mitigations include rate limiting, breached-password
screening, hardware-backed factors, short token lifetimes and automated secret scanning
of all repositories on every push.

5. Authorization
Authorization decisions are enforced server-side on every request. Client-side checks are
treated as user-interface hints only. Roles are stored in a dedicated authorization
service and never in a user profile record, preventing privilege escalation through
profile edits. Every authorization denial is logged with the subject, resource and policy
that produced the decision.

6. Logging and Monitoring
Security-relevant events are shipped to the central log platform within 60 seconds and
retained for 400 days. Logs must never contain passwords, tokens, or complete customer
records. Alerting thresholds are reviewed monthly by the detection engineering team.

7. Incident Response
7.1 Severity Levels
Incidents are graded S1 to S4. An S1 incident is a confirmed compromise of Restricted
data or a full production outage.

7.2 Response Timelines
An on-call responder must acknowledge an S1 incident within 15 minutes and declare an
incident channel within 30 minutes. Customer notification for confirmed unauthorised
access to Restricted data occurs within 72 hours of confirmation.

7.3 Authentication-Related Incidents
Any incident involving suspected credential compromise triggers mandatory session
revocation for the affected accounts, forced re-enrolment of authentication factors, and a
review of the access entitlements held by those accounts. The incident report must state
whether multi-factor authentication was enforced on the affected path; if it was not, a
remediation task is opened with a 30-day deadline.

7.4 Post-Incident Review
A blameless review is completed within ten business days for all S1 and S2 incidents.
Actions are tracked to closure and reported to the Security Review Board.

8. Vendor and Third-Party Risk
Vendors handling Confidential or Restricted data complete a security assessment before
contract signature and annually thereafter. Contracts must include breach notification
within 24 hours and a right to audit.

9. Security Recommendations
The Security Review Board recommends prioritising phishing-resistant multi-factor
authentication for all staff, eliminating standing production access, expanding automated
secret scanning to build artefacts, and rehearsing the incident response plan twice per
year with a credential-compromise scenario.
